How carpet and pad respond to water

Carpet is a layered system: the surface fibers, a backing, a pad underneath and then the subfloor. The pad is a sponge, and the backing keeps water from draining away. A spill that looks like a damp patch on top can mean a saturated pad underneath and wet concrete or plywood below that.

Whether carpet is saved depends mainly on the water category and on time. Clean water from a supply line, extracted quickly, often leaves a carpet that can be dried in place. Contaminated water or a long soak usually leads to removal.

What to do first

  1. Stop the source and remove standing water with a wet vacuum if the area is small and the water is clean.
  2. Lift furniture off wet carpet and place foil or plastic under the legs.
  3. Peel back a corner only if you can do it safely, and do not walk on the wet area more than necessary.
  4. Photograph the room and ask whether the water was clean or contaminated.
  5. Call (727) 626-0985 to arrange extraction and drying.

How carpet drying is done

  1. Extract thoroughly

    A weighted extractor presses water out of the carpet and pad in overlapping passes.

  2. Lift and float

    The carpet is lifted from the tack strip, and the pad is removed or the carpet is floated on air so the subfloor can dry.

  3. Dry and measure

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run, and moisture meters check the carpet, pad area and subfloor.

  4. Clean and reinstall

    Carpet that can be saved is cleaned, deodorized and restretched. New pad goes in.

Carpet and slab homes in Seminole

Most Seminole homes sit on slabs, and carpet often lies directly over concrete. Water spreading across a slab travels under the pad, and concrete holds moisture for days. Bedrooms and hallways in 1970s to 1990s homes tend to have carpet laid on slab with a thin pad. If a leak starts in a bathroom or laundry closet, the water can reach several rooms before you see it.

Wet carpet in a humid climate can smell sour within a day and can host mold in the pad. Our blog on how long mold takes to grow after water damage explains the timeline, and our mold remediation page covers what happens if growth is present.

Cost factors and insurance

The price depends on the area, whether the pad is replaced, the water category, and how many drying days follow. If carpet must be replaced, matching a specific style may take time. Insurance often covers sudden water events and often excludes gradual seepage. Check your policy and ask your insurer, and see our guide on how long water damage takes to dry.
