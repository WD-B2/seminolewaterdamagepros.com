Commercial water damage is a scheduling problem as well as a drying problem

A leak in a store or office stops revenue, puts stock at risk and may affect neighbors. The goal of commercial restoration is to stop the spread, protect what can be saved and get the space open again, in that order. The drying science is the same as in homes, but the logistics are heavier because of larger floor areas, shared walls, roofs and ceilings, and tenant and landlord responsibilities.

Seminole's commercial corridor runs along Park Boulevard, Seminole Boulevard and Alternate US 19. Strip centers and medical offices share roof structures, so a leak from one unit often reaches the next.

Emergency steps for a business water loss

  1. Shut off the water source if you can, or ask the building manager to do so.
  2. Move inventory, records and electronics off the floor and away from the wet ceiling.
  3. Photograph the loss and list affected stock and equipment.
  4. Call the property manager or landlord, who often controls the building systems and the insurance for the structure.
  5. Call (727) 626-0985 so the crew can plan extraction, drying and any phased reopening.

The line is answered 24/7, and same-day response is available when a crew is free.

How we plan a commercial job

  1. Triage the building

    We map wet areas, locate the source and identify shared structure, utilities and neighboring tenants.

  2. Protect critical items

    Inventory, records and equipment are moved or covered. Work areas are separated from open areas where possible.

  3. Extract and dry

    Larger extractors and multiple dehumidifiers dry floors, ceilings and wall bases while readings are logged.

  4. Phase the work

    Drying is staged so part of the space can reopen early when safe.

  5. Document

    Moisture logs, photos and a scope help the owner, tenant and insurers.

Who pays and who decides

In leased space, the lease and the landlord's policy decide who handles the building and who handles contents and business interruption. Tenants often carry their own coverage for stock, equipment and lost income. Check your policy wording, ask your agent early and keep a list of what was lost, because the claim process rewards good records.

Our post on water damage insurance claim steps is written for homeowners, but the documentation habits apply to businesses too.

Commercial jobs are priced by affected area, the hours of extraction, the equipment and days of drying and the amount of ceiling, wall and floor material that must come out. A walk-through is needed before an honest number can be given.

Seminole City Center, the strip centers along Park Boulevard and the medical offices near Bay Pines draw a mix of tenants. Roofs on older plazas are a frequent source of leaks during summer storms. If your unit shares a roof or wall with others, tell the dispatcher so the plan covers the neighbors too.

We dispatch from Park Boulevard North across Seminole and to commercial areas in Indian Rocks Beach and Saint Petersburg.

Commercial drying follows the same ANSI/IICRC S500 principles as residential work, scaled up for larger floor areas and shared structure.

Safety steps and what to do before the crew arrives

  • Evacuate anyone from rooms with sagging ceilings or water near live electrical equipment.
  • Keep customers and staff out of wet areas and place wet-floor signs.
  • Do not move heavy inventory through standing water.
  • Leave the building and call 911 or the utility if you smell gas.
