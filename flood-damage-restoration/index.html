Why flood damage is a different job

Floodwater comes from outside the plumbing system. It carries soil, bacteria, fuel, fertilizer and debris, so the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard treats it as Category 3 water. Porous materials that touched it, including carpet, pad, insulation and drywall, usually cannot be cleaned safely and come out. Hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected.

The CDC advises that people avoid contact with floodwater, wear waterproof boots and gloves, and assume anything that sat in it for more than a day or two needs careful cleaning or removal. Children and pets should stay out of the affected rooms.

Emergency flood response: safe first steps

  1. Stay out of rising water and leave if you are told to evacuate. Never drive or walk through moving floodwater.
  2. Do not touch electrical equipment while standing in water. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the utility.
  3. Photograph the high-water line and damaged contents before cleanup begins.
  4. Do not run a regular household vacuum on wet carpet.
  5. Once it is safe, call (727) 626-0985 so we can plan extraction and protective equipment.

The dispatch line is answered 24/7. After a major storm many neighbors call at once, so the dispatcher will give you an honest read on scheduling rather than a promise.

What flood restoration involves

  1. Safety check

    Crews check for electrical and structural hazards before work begins and wear gloves, boots and respiratory protection as needed.

  2. Extract and remove

    Water is pumped out. Carpet, pad, insulation and wet drywall that touched floodwater are removed and bagged.

  3. Clean and disinfect

    Hard surfaces and framing are cleaned, then treated with an appropriate disinfectant.

  4. Dry and verify

    Air movers and dehumidifiers dry the structure, and moisture readings confirm the result.

  5. Repair

    Drywall, insulation, flooring and paint are rebuilt after the structure is dry.

Our water extraction page covers the first stage, and the sewage backup page covers the cleanup rules for contaminated interior water.

Flood exposure in the Seminole area

Seminole sits in Pinellas County, which is surrounded by tidal water and low ground. Lake Seminole, Long Bayou and Boca Ciega Bay shape where water collects, and heavy rain can overwhelm drainage on low streets. Tropical systems add storm surge risk on coastal parcels. Flood zones vary block by block, so check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your own address or ask the county building department.

The city and county can both issue guidance after a storm, and Pinellas County publishes evacuation zone information before major hurricanes. Know your zone before the season starts.

Flood insurance and cost factors

Standard homeowners insurance usually excludes flood damage, which is why a separate flood policy exists. If you have one, report the loss promptly and keep photos and receipts. If you do not, ask your insurer what applies to storm-driven rain, which is often handled differently. Our blog on whether homeowners insurance covers water damage explains the common wording.

The price of flood restoration depends on the square footage reached, the depth and duration of the water, how much porous material must be removed and how long drying runs. A thorough inspection before rebuild matters, because mold can follow, which is why we also cover mold remediation.
