Water damage restoration in Seminole, FL: what the first 72 hours look like

Water damage restoration is the work of removing water, drying every wet material, cleaning what the water touched and repairing what could not be saved. In Seminole it usually runs in that order: water extraction first, then structural drying and dehumidification, then repairs. Seminole Water Damage Pros dispatches that work around the clock for homes and businesses in Seminole, Indian Rocks Beach and Saint Petersburg.

The clock matters because of our climate. The National Weather Service station in St. Petersburg records about 47 inches of rain in an average year, and Gulf Coast air stays humid for most of it. Wet drywall, carpet pad and insulation do not dry on their own in that air, and CDC guidance says porous items that stay wet beyond 48 hours usually have to be removed instead of saved.

While you wait for the crew, shut off the water source, switch off power to wet areas only if the panel is dry and easy to reach, and lift small furniture off wet floors. Take photos of everything before you move it, because your insurer will ask for them. Never walk through standing water near outlets, and call the utility or 911 for any electrical or gas concern before you call us.

What affects the cost, and how insurance fits

Water damage restoration cost depends on five things. The first is whether the water is clean, gray or contaminated. The others are how many square feet are wet, which materials absorbed it, how many days of drying equipment the job needs and how much rebuild work follows. Nobody can price those from a phone description. The dispatcher explains the scope first, and you decide from there.

Sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst supply line or a failed water heater, is often covered by a homeowners policy. Gradual leaks and neglect often are not, and flood damage is usually excluded and needs its own flood policy. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and see our pages on flood damage cleanup and burst pipe damage for how each source is handled.
