Kitchen Water Damage Restoration in Seminole, FL

Kitchen leaks soak cabinet bases, toe kicks and the subfloor underneath. We dry or replace what the water reached and repair the finish.

Why kitchens are the highest risk room

A kitchen packs more water connections into one room than any other. Sink supply lines, a dishwasher, a refrigerator ice-maker, a disposal and often a pot-filler or beverage tap all sit near cabinets and flooring. A leak under the sink drips onto cabinet bases made of particleboard, which swell and break down when wet.

Cabinets also hide leaks. By the time a toe kick darkens or a drawer sticks, the subfloor under the cabinets may be soaked.

Because kitchens often adjoin living areas with hardwood floors or carpet, the water often travels past the cabinets.

What to do when a kitchen leak starts

  1. Close the shutoff valve under the sink or behind the appliance, or the home's main valve.
  2. Unplug the dishwasher and refrigerator if water is near their cords, and stay out of standing water near outlets.
  3. Empty the cabinet and move items to a dry place.
  4. Photograph the leak, cabinet and floor before you clean.
  5. Call (727) 626-0985 so we can map the moisture before the cabinet base is replaced.

The dispatch line is answered 24/7. A kitchen leak is often urgent, especially when water reaches the floor, and the dispatcher will treat it that way.

How restoration works in a kitchen

  1. Map the moisture

    A meter and thermal camera trace water through the floor, cabinet bases and adjoining walls.

  2. Open the wet cavities

    Toe kicks and the lowest drawers come off, and small holes allow airflow behind cabinets.

  3. Dry

    Air movers and a dehumidifier run, sometimes with a specialty mat or cavity drying system for the subfloor.

  4. Repair

    Cabinet bases, flooring, drywall and paint are replaced where drying could not save them.

Underneath, the process is the same as structural drying, but access is trickier because the cabinets are fixed in place.

Common kitchen leaks in Seminole homes

Many Seminole kitchens were built in the 1970s through the 1990s and have original shutoff valves that stick and supply lines that have aged. A dishwasher installed with a rubber hose or a refrigerator with a plastic ice-maker tube is a classic failure point. Condo kitchens in the lake-side communities share stack lines with neighbors, so a leak from above can arrive through the ceiling.

Replacing old braided supply lines and testing the shutoff valves every year is a sensible habit. Our blog on how to check for water damage before buying a home also lists signs to look for in a kitchen.

Cost factors and insurance

Kitchen jobs vary with how much cabinet base and flooring must be replaced, how many days of drying follow and whether finishes must match. Sudden leaks are often covered by homeowners insurance, while gradual leaks and wear often are not. Ask your insurer, and see our post on whether to file a claim for a small leak.
