Signs of mold after water damage: musty odor, staining and fuzzy patches

  • A musty or earthy smell that returns after cleaning.
  • Dark, green, white or black spots on walls, ceilings or baseboards.
  • Paint that bubbles or wallpaper that peels near a past leak.
  • Fuzzy growth on the back of drywall, under carpet or on cabinet bases.
  • Condensation on windows or ducts and a damp feel in a closed room.

Surface discoloration does not always mean mold, and mold can hide behind walls. A trained assessment and, where appropriate, testing help show what is there. Our blog on how long mold takes to grow after water damage explains the timeline.

Moisture comes first

Mold needs moisture, food and the right temperature. In Pinellas County the temperature and humidity are favorable for most of the year, so the only practical control is moisture. The EPA states that mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours and that the key to control is controlling moisture. Remediation that does not fix the source simply returns.

That is why mold work at our company sits behind water extraction and structural drying. If the building is still wet, the drying plan comes before anything else.

How remediation works

  1. Assess and plan

    The affected area is mapped, moisture sources are identified and a written scope is prepared.

  2. Contain

    Plastic sheeting and negative air machines keep spores from spreading to clean rooms.

  3. Remove

    Contaminated porous materials such as drywall, insulation and carpet are removed and bagged. Salvageable hard surfaces are cleaned.

  4. Clean the air and surfaces

    HEPA vacuuming and damp wiping remove settled particles.

  5. Verify

    A visual check and, where requested, independent post-remediation testing confirm the work.

The ANSI/IICRC S520 standard is the published reference for professional mold remediation. We follow its approach to containment, removal and verification.

Cleaning small areas yourself and when to stop

A small patch on a hard surface may be cleaned by a homeowner wearing gloves, eye protection and a mask. Our guides on cleaning mold off walls and removing mold naturally explain what is reasonable. Stop and call for help if the area is larger than a few square feet, if it keeps returning, if it is inside walls or the air system, or if water or sewage caused it.

Questions about health effects of mold belong with a physician. We do not make health claims. Our work is to find the moisture and remove the growth.

Local factors in Seminole

The Gulf Coast climate favors growth, and houses with closed windows, air conditioning running irregularly and poor ventilation in bathrooms and laundry rooms are most at risk. Seminole homes with slab floors can trap moisture under flooring, and attic leaks can feed growth in insulation. After a storm, check ceilings, closets and wall bases within the first days.

Cost factors and insurance

Mold cost depends on the size of the area, the building materials involved, the containment level, the testing required and the rebuild. See our post on mold removal cost for the general drivers. Insurance varies widely. Many policies limit or exclude mold unless it results from a covered water event, so check your policy and ask your insurer.

Safety steps before remediation begins

  • Stay out of the affected room and keep the door closed.
  • Turn off the air handler if it serves the area so spores are not spread.
  • Do not dry-scrub or sand moldy material, which puts dust in the air.
  • Wear an N95 respirator, gloves and eye protection if you must enter.
  • Ask a physician about any health concern.
