Why Florida AC systems leak so often

Air conditioners remove moisture as well as heat. In Pinellas County the humidity is high for much of the year, so a system can produce gallons of condensate every day. That water leaves the air handler through a drain line, and when the line clogs with algae, dust or debris, the drain pan fills, overflows and drips into whatever is below.

Air handlers are often in attics, closets or garages, so overflow lands on insulation, ceilings and floors. A rusted pan, a cracked drain fitting or a frozen evaporator coil can also release water.

What to do when your AC is leaking

  1. Turn the system off at the thermostat and, if you can reach it safely, at the breaker. A running unit keeps producing water.
  2. Place a bucket under active drips and move anything valuable out of the way.
  3. Check the drain line outlet outside, which is usually a PVC pipe near the condenser, and the pan under the air handler for standing water.
  4. Call an HVAC technician to clear or replace the drain line and repair the cause.
  5. Call (727) 626-0985 so we can dry the wet areas. Take photos of the stain and the air handler first.

The dispatch line is open 24/7. This service is not always an emergency, but a sagging ceiling or active drip is, and the dispatcher will treat it that way.

How we restore AC water damage

AC overflow is usually clean condensate, though it can pick up dust and biological growth from the pan. The ceiling is the usual casualty. Crews cut away wet or sagging drywall, remove saturated insulation, dry the cavity and then repair and repaint. If the water reached a closet or hall floor, we dry the flooring too.

Why an AC leak is easy to underestimate

Because AC leaks are slow and the water is mostly clean, people often leave them alone for weeks. Insulation stays damp, drywall softens and a closet floor may stay wet. In a humid climate that wet material is a good place for mold.

If you see a stain below an air handler or smell something sour in a closet, have the area checked before repainting. Our guide on how long mold takes to grow after water damage explains the timing.

A simple prevention habit helps: have an HVAC technician flush the drain line during seasonal service and consider a float switch that shuts the system off if the pan fills. Both are cheaper than drywall.

Seminole details

Seminole homes from the 1970s through the 2000s, including those near Park Boulevard and Seminole Boulevard, often have air handlers in the attic or in a hall closet. Attic handlers drain into a pan over the ceiling, so a clog can stain a hallway ceiling. If you rent or live in a condo, tell the property manager, because the unit may drain into a shared space.

Cost factors and insurance

Repair cost depends on how much drywall and insulation is wet, whether flooring is involved and how long drying takes. Insurance often treats a sudden overflow differently than a long-term drip, so describe the timeline honestly to your insurer. Read the policy wording first, and see our guide to filing a claim for a small water leak.
