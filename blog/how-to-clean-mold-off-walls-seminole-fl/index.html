How to Clean Mold Off Walls in a Seminole, FL Home

Small patches on hard surfaces can be cleaned carefully. Larger growth, porous walls and hidden mold need a professional.

Written by the Seminole Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026.

Call (727) 626-0985

First decide whether it is a DIY job

Cleaning mold off walls is reasonable only in narrow cases. The EPA notes that small areas of mold can often be handled by building occupants, while larger areas call for professional help. A rough guide is a patch smaller than about 10 square feet, on a hard surface, with no sign of ongoing water.

If the wall is soft, bubbling or crumbling, or the growth is behind the surface, cleaning the face will not solve the problem.

Fix the moisture before you clean

Cleaning without fixing the leak or humidity problem only buys a few weeks. Find where the water comes from. It might be a leaking supply line, an AC drain pan, a roof flashing leak, a window seal or condensation from an under-ventilated bathroom. A plumber or HVAC technician can fix the source, and we can dry the area. See our pages on AC leaks and wall and drywall water damage.

These steps follow EPA guidelines for small mold problems, which favor detergent and water on hard surfaces and fixing the moisture source first.

Protect yourself first

  • Wear an N95 respirator, gloves and eye protection without vents.
  • Open a window if it is safe and shut the door to keep dust out of other rooms.
  • Switch off the air handler so it does not spread spores.
  • Keep children, pets and anyone with breathing problems out of the area.

Do not mix bleach with ammonia or other cleaners, since that can release toxic gas. We make no health claims, and anyone who feels unwell should speak to a physician.

Cleaning steps for a small hard-surface patch

  1. Lightly mist the area

    A light mist of water keeps dust and spores down when you wipe.

  2. Scrub with detergent and water

    Use a soft brush and a mild detergent. The EPA says detergent and water are enough for most hard surfaces.

  3. Rinse and wipe

    Wipe away residue with clean water and a disposable cloth.

  4. Dry completely

    Use a fan and a dehumidifier. A damp wall will grow mold again.

  5. Dispose of materials

    Bag rags and sponges and take them out of the house.

Bleach is not required for most cases and does not remove the cause. It also can damage some surfaces. Our guide on removing mold naturally covers the alternatives people ask about.

When to stop and call a professional

  • The patch is larger than a few square feet or spreads while you clean.
  • The wall is soft, swollen or stained from inside.
  • You find growth behind baseboards, wallpaper or cabinets.
  • The musty smell remains after cleaning.
  • The cause was sewage, flood or a long-term leak.
  • Someone in the home is sensitive or has a health concern.

Professionals work under containment and remove porous materials, as described on our mold remediation page. Drying comes first, which is what structural drying covers.

Painting over a cleaned patch is fine only after the surface is completely dry and the cause is fixed. A stain-blocking primer can help hide residual discoloration, but it is cosmetic and not a treatment.

After cleaning: confirm the area stays dry

Check the spot again after a week and again after the next heavy rain. If the wall is cool and damp, or the smell returns, moisture is still there. A moisture meter reading against a dry wall of the same material gives a clear answer. Many homeowners borrow or buy an inexpensive meter, and our crews can take readings during an assessment.

Seminole and Pinellas notes

Humidity is the background condition here. Bathroom fans that vent into the attic instead of outdoors, closets on exterior walls, and AC systems that cycle on and off in humid weather all create damp surfaces. Run bathroom fans after showers, keep indoor humidity in a moderate range, and check closets and corners after storms.

If you live near the water in Indian Rocks Beach or in an older house in Saint Petersburg, check window sills, baseboards and any wall that shares a plumbing run.
