Water Damage Categories 1, 2 and 3 Explained for Seminole, FL Homeowners

The category of water decides what can be dried and what must be thrown out. Here is the plain-language version of the ANSI/IICRC S500 system.

Written by the Seminole Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026.

Call (727) 626-0985

Why categories exist

Not all water is equally dangerous. A supply line break puts clean drinking water on your floor, and a sewer backup puts waste on it. Restoration professionals sort water into three categories under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard so that cleanup matches the risk. The category sets safety measures, which materials can be dried in place and which are removed, and how thoroughly surfaces are cleaned.

The three water categories.
CategorySource typeExamplesRisk level
Category 1Clean waterSupply line, sink overflow with clean water, rain from a roof openingLow at first.
Category 2Gray water, some contaminationWasher or dishwasher discharge, toilet overflow without feces, aquariumModerate.
Category 3Grossly contaminatedSewage, floodwater, rising surface water, standing water with microbesHigh.

Category 1: clean water

This comes from a sanitary source. A burst supply line, a leaking water heater or a failed fridge ice-maker line all start as Category 1. Carpet, pad and drywall often can be dried if the water is removed quickly. The danger is time. Clean water in contact with building materials picks up contaminants, and the category can worsen.

Category 2: gray water

Gray water has some contamination that could cause discomfort or illness. Washing machine overflow, dishwasher discharge and a toilet overflow with urine but no feces are typical. Porous materials often come out, and hard surfaces are cleaned. Time makes it worse too, and Category 2 water can turn into Category 3.

Category 3: grossly contaminated

Sewage, river or bay water, and floodwater are in this group, as is water that has stood so long that growth has developed. Carpet, pad, insulation and drywall that touched it are removed. Hard surfaces are cleaned and disinfected, with protective equipment and containment. See our pages on sewage backup and flood damage. The CDC advises people to avoid contact with floodwater and sewage.

How water moves between categories

  • Time: clean water left in materials can degrade after about 48 hours.
  • Contact: water passing through dirty insulation or soil picks up contaminants.
  • Temperature: warmth speeds microbial growth.
  • Source change: a clean line that flows through a dirty area becomes Category 2 or 3.

What you can do

  1. Learn the source: ask where the water came from and how long it has been there.
  2. Keep people and pets out of anything Category 2 or 3.
  3. Do not use a household vacuum on wet floors.
  4. Call us so the category can be set and the right plan chosen.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is the published reference, and categories also affect insurance, as described on our homepage and service pages.

Category versus class of water loss

Categories describe how contaminated the water is. A separate idea, the class of water loss, describes how much of a building is wet and how absorbent the materials are, from a small area with low absorbency to a large loss with deeply saturated materials.

Both ideas appear in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Together they shape the plan: the category sets safety and what is removed, and the class sets how much drying equipment is needed.

In practice a homeowner needs only the headline. Ask the crew what category they assigned and why, and ask how much of the structure is wet. Those two answers explain most of the work and most of the price.

Why you should not guess at the category

Water that looks clear can be contaminated, and water that looks dirty can be less risky than it seems. Color and smell are only clues. Source, time and path matter more. For that reason a trained technician assigns the category on site, and a homeowner who suspects sewage or flood water should treat the area as Category 3 until someone has checked.

Seminole and Pinellas notes

Barrier island homes near Indian Rocks Beach and low neighborhoods in Saint Petersburg are the clearest examples. Inland Seminole sees more supply line and appliance events, which start as Category 1.
