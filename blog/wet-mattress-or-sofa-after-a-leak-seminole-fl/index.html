What to Do With a Wet Mattress or Sofa After a Leak

Mattresses and upholstery absorb water and dry slowly. Whether to save them depends on the water type and how fast you act.

Written by the Seminole Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026.

Call (727) 626-0985

First, what kind of water was it

The water type decides almost everything. Clean water from a supply line or a roof leak that was caught early gives you a chance to save a sofa or mattress. Water from a sewer backup, a flood or a dirty source is Category 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, and upholstered items that absorbed it are normally discarded. When in doubt, ask whether the water could have carried contamination.

What to do in the first hours

  1. Stop the water and move the item out of the wet area.
  2. Lift it off the floor. Wet items sitting on wet carpet keep absorbing.
  3. Photograph it for your insurance file before doing anything else.
  4. Blot, do not rub. Press clean towels into the surface to pull water out.
  5. Move it to a dry, well-ventilated spot. A dehumidifier and fan help more than sunshine in a humid place.
  6. Call us if the room itself is wet so the structure can be dried too.

The dispatch line is open 24/7. Items are secondary to the room, since a wet floor and wall are what damage the house.

Drying a mattress

Mattress foam and springs hold water deep inside, and the surface can feel drier than the core. Memory foam and latex absorb a lot. If you decide to try drying, stand it on its edge, blot as much as you can, and use a dehumidifier and fan in a closed room.

Do not use a hair dryer on foam, and do not sleep on a mattress that smells sour. If the mattress was soaked or the smell persists after a day or two, replacement is usually the safer choice.

Drying a sofa or upholstered chair

Fabric, padding and wood frames each dry differently. Remove cushions, blot, and place the pieces on edge or on a rack so air reaches all sides. Wood frames can swell, and the padding underneath can hold moisture. Professional upholstery cleaning can help with clean-water events, but contaminated water means disposal.

When mold becomes the concern

Wet materials in warm humid air can begin to grow mold within 24 to 48 hours, according to the EPA. That is faster than most people expect. If an item is still damp after two days, or smells musty, it is usually better to remove it from the home. Our guide to mold remediation covers the next step if growth reaches the walls or floor.

Insurance and contents

Contents coverage is separate from structure coverage on most policies. Keep photos, receipts and a list of items with approximate age. Do not dispose of damaged contents until the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe or contaminated.

Whatever you decide about the item, make sure the room under it is dry. See our carpet water damage page and our page on structural drying for how that is checked.

For irreplaceable items such as photos and documents, separate them, keep them flat and dry them away from heat. Professional document drying exists for valuable pieces.

Setting up a drying space

Pick a room with air conditioning, lay plastic or towels on the floor and stand items so air can reach all sides. A dehumidifier in the same room speeds drying far more than a window fan in humid weather. Check items each day and discard anything that smells sour after two or three days.
