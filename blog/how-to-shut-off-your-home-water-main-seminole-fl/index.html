How to Shut Off Your Home Water Main in Seminole, FL

Knowing where the main valve is and how to close it is the fastest way to limit water damage. Here is how to find and test it.

Written by the Seminole Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026.

Call (727) 626-0985

Why this is the first thing to learn

A burst pipe, a failed hose or a ruptured water heater keeps pouring until someone stops it. Every minute you spend searching for a valve is water on the floor. Finding the shutoff before an emergency is the cheapest step you can take to protect your home. Do it today, and test the valve once a year.

Where the shutoff valves usually are

Typical valve locations.

In Florida homes the supply usually enters through the slab or an exterior wall, not through a basement. Follow the pipe from the meter toward the house, or look near the water heater.

Utility guidelines differ on who may operate the valve at the meter, so check your utility's rules before using it. Your own house valve is always the first choice.

Valve types and how to close them

  • Ball valve: a lever handle. A quarter turn so the handle sits across the pipe closes it.
  • Gate valve: a round wheel handle. Turn clockwise until it stops, without forcing it.
  • Meter-side valve: may need a tool, and the utility may prefer you to use your own house valve.
  • Fixture shutoffs: turn clockwise to close, and keep them operated once a year so they do not seize.

Step by step in an emergency

  1. Close the main

    Turn the house valve to closed. If it will not move easily, do not force it hard enough to snap the stem.

  2. Drain the lines

    Open a faucet at the lowest point and one upstairs to relieve pressure.

  3. Shut off the water heater

    Turn off its power or gas so it does not run dry.

  4. Confirm the flow has stopped

    Check the meter. If the dial is still moving, the shutoff has not worked.

  5. Call for help

    Call a plumber for the repair and our line for water extraction.

  6. Photograph the damage

    Take photos before moving anything.

If the valve is stuck, leaking or missing

A valve that has not moved in years may seize. Do not hit it with tools. Call a plumber to replace it, and ask for a full-port ball valve, which is easier to operate and more reliable than an old gate valve.

If you cannot find any house valve, ask your utility how to shut off at the meter, or ask a plumber to install one. Some utilities will close the meter in an emergency.

After the water is off

Stopping the flow is the start. Standing water must still be extracted, and wet materials must be dried, because mold can begin in 24 to 48 hours. Our pages on burst pipes and water heater leaks explain the cleanup for the most common failures, and the structural drying page explains how readings guide the finish.

Make it routine

  • Label the main valve with a tag so anyone in the house can find it.
  • Test the valve once a year and note which direction closes it.
  • Replace braided washer hoses on a schedule.
  • Keep the plumber's number near the valve.
  • Show everyone in the house, including renters and guests staying a long time.

Valve problems worth fixing before an emergency

Old valves fail in two ways. They seize so that the handle will not turn, or they turn and do not close, because the washer inside has worn out. Test yours by closing it and then opening a faucet downstream.

If water still runs freely, the valve is not sealing. A plumber can replace a main valve in a short visit, and the cost is small compared with a flooded floor.

  • Replace gate valves that feel stiff or leak at the stem.
  • Choose full-port ball valves for new installs.
  • Add an accessible shutoff if the only valve is at the street.
  • Consider an automatic leak detector with a shutoff for long trips.
  • Label the valve and keep the area around it clear.

A leak detection system with an automatic shutoff valve can close the supply when it senses abnormal flow. These systems range from simple to elaborate, and a plumber can advise which suits your house.

Seminole and Pinellas notes

Most Seminole homes sit on slabs, so supply lines run in or under the concrete, and the main entry is often near an outside wall or the water heater. Condo units, especially in buildings near Park Boulevard and the lake-side communities, may have a unit shutoff and a separate building riser valve that only management can close. Ask your association where the unit valve is.

In Indian Rocks Beach and Saint Petersburg, older homes may have a rusted shutoff, so check it before you need it.
