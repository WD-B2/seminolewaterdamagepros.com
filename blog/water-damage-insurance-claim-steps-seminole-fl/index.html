Water Damage Insurance Claim Steps for Seminole, FL Homeowners

A clear order of operations can keep a claim organized. This guide covers what to do from the first minute to the final repair.

Written by the Seminole Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026.

Call (727) 626-0985

Why order matters in a water claim

A claim is easier to settle when the evidence is clear. Water loss changes quickly, because wet materials degrade, mold can start within 24 to 48 hours and the room you photograph today looks different tomorrow. Doing things in the right order protects your property and your paper trail.

We restore water damage and do not adjust claims, so treat this as a practical checklist and follow your own policy and insurer for specifics.

Step by step

  1. Make it safe and stop the water

    Close the fixture shutoff or the main valve. If outlets are wet, switch off power only from a dry panel, or call the utility.

  2. Document before you clean

    Take wide photos, close-ups and video of the source, the damage and each affected item. Write down the time you found it.

  3. Start mitigation

    Call a restoration company to extract water and begin drying. Most policies expect you to prevent further damage, and the receipts are usually part of the claim.

  4. Notify your insurer

    Report the loss promptly by phone or the insurer's app. Ask for a claim number and the adjuster's contact.

  5. Make a contents list

    List damaged belongings with age, brand and estimated value. Keep damaged items until the adjuster approves disposal, unless they are unsafe.

  6. Meet the adjuster

    Walk the adjuster through the damage and share your photos, moisture logs and drying scope.

  7. Review the estimate

    Compare the insurer's scope with the restoration scope. If they differ, ask in writing what is included and why.

  8. Complete repairs and keep records

    Save invoices, change orders and final photos until the claim is closed.

Insurer guidelines for documentation and timing differ, so read your policy's duties after loss and ask your adjuster what format they prefer.

Common mistakes

  • Throwing out damaged items before photographing them.
  • Waiting days to start drying, which lets damage grow.
  • Signing a contract or assignment before reading it.
  • Assuming a small leak is not worth reporting, which our guide on whether to file a claim for a small leak answers.

On the last item, ask your insurer or agent and keep it factual.

Different water, different path

The type of loss changes the steps. A sudden burst pipe is usually handled as a plumbing discharge. A flood calls for a flood policy claim. A sewage backup may require a backup endorsement. If your policy has a named storm deductible, wind-driven rain from a hurricane may fall under it. Ask early which category your loss falls in.

Working with the adjuster

The adjuster represents the insurer. Be polite, be organized and answer questions factually. Do not guess about when something started, and say so if you do not know. If you disagree with a decision, ask for the reasoning in writing, and ask what appeals or re-inspection options your policy offers. You may hire a public adjuster or an attorney if you want help, but we cannot advise you on that.

Keeping an organized claim file

A claim can run for weeks, and details get lost. Keep one folder for everything, either a paper binder or a shared digital folder. Put the claim number on a front sheet with the adjuster's name and phone.

File every email, estimate, invoice, photo set and note from every call. After each call, write down the date, the person and what they said, and send a short follow-up email so the conversation is on record.

  • Photos and video taken before any cleanup.
  • The restoration scope, moisture log and drying log.
  • Plumber or roofer invoices that identify the cause.
  • A contents list with brand, age and approximate value.
  • Receipts for emergency costs such as hotel stays or equipment.
  • Every estimate, even ones you do not accept.

If living in the home is impossible during repairs, ask whether your policy includes additional living expense coverage and what proof it requires. Save receipts for hotel and food costs above your normal spending.

A Seminole homeowner checklist

Keep a small folder, paper or digital, with your policy declarations page, the shutoff valve location, the plumber and restoration numbers and photos of each room from a normal day. A baseline set of photos makes before-and-after comparison simple.
