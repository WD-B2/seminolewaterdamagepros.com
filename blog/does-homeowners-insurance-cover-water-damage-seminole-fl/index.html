Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Water Damage in Seminole, FL?

It depends on how the water got in. Sudden burst pipes are often covered, while flood and slow leaks often are not.

Written by the Seminole Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026.

Call (727) 626-0985

The short answer: it depends on the cause

Homeowners policies are written around causes of loss, not around the word water. Many standard policies cover water damage that is sudden and accidental, like a supply line that bursts or a water heater that ruptures. Many exclude damage that happens slowly, such as a drip behind a wall that runs for months, and they usually exclude flood.

That is a general pattern, not a promise, and the wording in your own policy controls.

We restore water damage and do not sell insurance, so this is not legal or insurance advice. Use it as a way to read your policy and ask better questions of your insurer or agent.

Causes that are often covered and often not

Florida homeowners policies also commonly have a separate and larger deductible for named storms, which affects wind-driven rain claims. Ask your agent how the hurricane deductible is calculated for your policy.

Terms to look for in your policy

  • Sudden and accidental discharge, which usually describes covered plumbing events.
  • Continuous or repeated seepage exclusions, commonly 14 days or longer, so check your own wording.
  • Water damage limits or sublimits on some policies.
  • Flood exclusions and flood policy definitions.
  • Water backup of sewers and drains endorsements.
  • Mold, fungi and wet rot limitations.
  • Duties after loss, including prompt notice and protecting the property.

The last item matters. Most policies expect you to take reasonable steps to prevent further damage, such as shutting off the water and arranging emergency drying. Keep receipts, since many policies reimburse reasonable emergency mitigation as part of a covered claim.

What to do first when water gets in

  1. Stop the source if you can do it safely, and see our guide on shutting off the water main only if you need it.
  2. Photograph and video everything before cleanup, including the source and any damaged belongings.
  3. Call us for water extraction and drying so the damage does not grow.
  4. Contact your insurer or agent promptly and ask what they need.
  5. Keep damaged items until the adjuster has seen them, unless they are unsafe.

Fast mitigation protects you in two ways. It reduces the damage, and it shows that you acted to limit the loss.

How the cause shapes the restoration plan too

Sources that insurers treat differently also call for different work. A clean supply-line failure is Category 1 and often leaves carpet that can be dried. A sewer backup is Category 3 and removes porous materials. Our pages on burst pipes, flood damage and sewage backup explain each path, and the documentation we keep, including photos, moisture readings and scope, helps your adjuster see the loss clearly.

Questions to ask your insurer

  • Is my loss considered sudden and accidental, and how do you define it.
  • What is my deductible for this type of loss.
  • Are mold costs covered, and is there a limit.
  • Do I need to use a particular contractor, and may I choose my own.
  • What documents do you need from me.

Write down the answers with the date and the name of the person who gave them.

Reading the fine print on gradual damage

The line between sudden and gradual is where most disputes start. A pipe that bursts at 2 a.m. is sudden. A fitting that weeps for three weeks behind a refrigerator is gradual, even if you only discovered it on a Tuesday. Policies differ on how long a leak must last before the seepage exclusion applies, and some carve out exceptions for hidden leaks inside walls or under slabs.

Your declarations page and the full policy form both matter, because endorsements can change the base wording.

If you are unsure how your insurer would classify a leak, say what you know and do not guess. An honest account such as the day you noticed the stain, the day the plumber found the cause and what the plumber said about duration is more useful than a confident story that later proves wrong.

Seminole and Pinellas notes

Pinellas County has a mix of 1970s to 1990s slab homes, older bungalows nearer Saint Petersburg and condo buildings along the barrier islands near Indian Rocks Beach. Condo owners often have an association master policy plus their own HO-6 policy, and the split between them depends on the documents. Coastal and low-lying addresses need a flood policy question answered separately, and the FEMA Flood Map Service Center shows the flood zone for any address.
