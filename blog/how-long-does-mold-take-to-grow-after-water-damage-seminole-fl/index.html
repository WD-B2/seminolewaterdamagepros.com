How Long Does Mold Take to Grow After Water Damage in Seminole, FL?

Mold can start within 24 to 48 hours on wet materials, and humid Florida air makes that window matter. Here is what affects the timing.

Written by the Seminole Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026.

Call (727) 626-0985

The 24 to 48 hour rule

The EPA says mold can begin growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours. That does not mean a room is full of mold on day two. It means the window for easy cleanup is short. Spores are already in the air and settle on surfaces, and they need only moisture, a food source such as wood, paper or dust, and a moderate temperature. Florida homes provide all three.

We do not make health claims about mold. If someone in the home is concerned, speak to a physician.

Real timing varies a lot. Cooler, drier conditions slow growth, and warm, humid conditions speed it up.

What changes the speed

  • Temperature: warm conditions favor growth.
  • Humidity: moisture in the air keeps materials damp.
  • Material: drywall paper, ceiling tile, carpet pad and untreated wood feed growth.
  • Water type: contaminated water adds nutrients.
  • Air movement: still air in a closed cavity stays damp.
  • How soon water was removed.

Why Florida homes are at higher risk

The Gulf Coast climate keeps indoor surfaces near the dew point for much of the year. An AC system that cycles on and off in humid weather leaves cold surfaces that sweat. A water event adds liquid water on top of that. After a storm, a leak in the ceiling, a slab leak or an AC overflow can leave insulation and drywall damp for days.

What to do in the first 48 hours

  1. Remove standing water

    Use water extraction to pull out liquid water.

  2. Remove what cannot dry

    Soaked pad, insulation and drywall are often removed.

  3. Dry the structure

    Dehumidifiers and air movers dry the cavity, per our structural drying process.

  4. Check hidden places

    Look behind baseboards, inside closets and under cabinets.

  5. Watch the smell

    A musty smell after drying points to moisture still in a material.

Signs mold may have started

  • A musty or earthy smell.
  • Dark, green or white spots on walls, ceilings or baseboards.
  • Paint that bubbles or peels near a past leak.
  • Fuzzy patches behind furniture or under carpet.
  • Allergy-like symptoms that someone ties to a specific room, which a physician can assess.

If you see growth or smell it after drying, the next step is assessment and, if needed, containment and removal. Our mold remediation page explains the process, and the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard is the published reference.

Notice that most of these are hidden. By the time you see growth, the hidden side is usually further along, which is why a moisture meter reading is more reliable than looking.

Slowing the clock

Anything that cuts the time materials stay wet cuts the risk. Fast extraction, removal of materials that cannot dry, steady dehumidification and moving air across surfaces all help. Keeping indoor temperatures cool and humidity lower also makes a difference. None of this guarantees that mold will not appear, but it moves the odds in your favor.

If you cannot tell whether a material is still wet, a moisture meter reading settles the question. Our crews take readings on every assessment, and you can ask for them whether or not you hire us. A reading near the dry reference is reassuring, and a high reading behind a wall tells you drying is not finished even if the surface feels dry.
