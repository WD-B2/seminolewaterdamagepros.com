How Much Does Water Damage Restoration Cost in Seminole, FL?

Price depends on the water category, the area, the materials and the drying days. This guide explains what to ask so quotes can be compared.

Written by the Seminole Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026.

Call (727) 626-0985

Why a flat number is not honest

Water damage jobs range from one wet corner to a whole floor. Restoration is priced by what has to be done, so the same square footage can cost very different amounts depending on the water type, the materials and the days of drying.

We do not post dollar ranges here, because a number without a scope misleads. What we can show you is how the price is built, so any quote you get is easy to read.

The five main drivers

What sets a restoration price.

Water categoryClean, gray or contaminatedSupply line vs sewage backup.

The water category in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard is one of the biggest price drivers, because Category 3 water adds removal, disposal and disinfection.

Safety steps while you wait for a quote

  • Shut off the water source and stay out of contaminated water.
  • Keep power off in wet areas unless the panel is dry and reachable.
  • Photograph the damage before you move anything.
  • Do not start tearing out materials before you have photos and a scope.

Emergency services versus rebuild

Restoration usually has two parts. The mitigation phase covers extraction, removal of wet materials, drying equipment and monitoring. The repair phase covers rebuilding what was removed. Some companies do both, and some hand off the rebuild. Ask whether a quote covers mitigation only or both, because a quote that looks low may leave out half the work.

What a written scope should include

  • The water category and the reason for it.
  • Measured areas and a moisture map.
  • Materials to be removed and materials to be dried.
  • Equipment list and expected days.
  • Daily monitoring and what ends the job.
  • Disposal and cleaning items.
  • What rebuild is included or excluded.
  • How changes to the scope are approved.

We explain scope and price before work starts, and we cannot give an honest number without hearing what is wet. Description first, then a plan.

Cost factors for specific problems

  • Burst pipes: length of time the water ran and number of rooms.
  • Sewage backups: contaminated material removal and disinfection.
  • Floods: porous material removal and longer drying.
  • Slab leaks: concrete drying time and flooring replacement.
  • Hardwood floors: drying in steps and refinishing.
  • Mold: containment, testing and rebuild.

Insurance and who pays

Many homeowners policies cover sudden and accidental water damage and often exclude gradual seepage, flood and sometimes sewer backup. Your deductible applies, and the insurer may pay the contractor or you. Ask your insurer how they pay, and keep photos, readings and invoices. If you are unsure whether to file, ask your agent before deciding.

Red flags in a quote

  • No site visit but a firm price.
  • A very low number with no scope.
  • Pressure to sign right now or to assign your insurance benefits.
  • No mention of moisture readings or drying targets.
  • No written scope or change-order process.

How to compare two quotes

Put the scopes side by side. Look at the number of rooms and square feet measured, whether the water category was identified, which materials will be removed, how many air movers and dehumidifiers are planned and how many monitoring visits are included.

A quote with fewer line items is not cheaper if it leaves out drying or disposal. A quote with more items is not better if the items are padding.

Ask both companies the same questions in the same order, and write down the answers. Ask how change orders work if hidden damage is found. In water damage jobs, surprises happen, and a clear process for approving added work protects you.

Where costs sometimes surprise people

  • Hidden wet insulation or framing found after walls are opened.
  • Asbestos or lead-paint testing on older homes before demolition.
  • Longer drying because of humid weather or dense materials.
  • Matching floors, cabinets or paint across rooms.
  • Contents cleaning or pack-out.

Ask also whether the company is paid directly by your insurer, by you or both, and what the payment schedule is. For larger jobs, a deposit and progress payments are common, and a written agreement should describe them. Never sign a blank form or a document you have not read, and keep a copy of everything you sign.

Why speed changes the price too

Cost grows with time. Water that is removed within a day leaves fewer wet materials, a smaller drying footprint and a smaller rebuild. Water that sits for several days soaks deeper into drywall, pad and framing, and some of it can degrade into a worse category.

Starting early is the one cost lever that is fully in your control, and it is why the first call matters more than the first quote.
