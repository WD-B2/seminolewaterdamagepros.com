Should You File a Claim for a Small Water Leak in Seminole, FL?

A small leak can still be a big decision. This guide lays out the trade-offs so you can have an informed talk with your agent.

Written by the Seminole Water Damage Pros team · Updated November 2026.

Call (727) 626-0985

Why the question is harder than it looks

A small leak feels like a problem you can handle yourself, and sometimes it is. But insurance decisions have consequences beyond the repair. A claim can raise your premium or affect renewal, and a missed claim can leave you paying for damage that spreads. The right answer depends on your deductible, the cost of the repair, your claims history and your policy wording.

We restore water damage and do not give insurance advice, so use this as a way to organize your thinking before you talk to your agent.

Many Florida policies have a deductible of a thousand dollars or more, and some have percentage deductibles for certain perils. A leak that costs less than your deductible is unlikely to produce a payment. A moisture assessment can tell you if the damage is bigger than it looks, which is why we map the moisture before we recommend anything.

Insurer guidelines on reporting and claim history differ, so ask your agent which rules apply to your policy before you decide.

What a claim can affect

  • Your premium at renewal, and in some cases whether the insurer renews you.
  • Your claims history in industry databases, which other insurers may check.
  • Your eligibility for discounts or certain carriers.
  • Your ability to claim later on the same cause, if the first repair was incomplete.

Practices differ among insurers, and some distinguish between a claim that is paid and a claim that is only reported or inquired about. Ask your agent how your insurer treats each, before you call the claims line.

What a missed claim can cost

Skipping a claim is not free. If a small leak turns out to have wet insulation, subfloor or framing, the repair cost can jump. Policies also expect prompt notice, and a late report can complicate a claim. If there is a chance the damage is larger than it looks, get an inspection and document everything before you decide.

A sensible way to decide

  1. Stop the leak and dry it

    Mitigation protects you whichever way you go. See our pages on water extraction and structural drying.

  2. Document

    Photos, moisture readings and a written scope make any later claim easier.

  3. Get the repair scope and cost

    A written estimate lets you compare the repair to your deductible.

  4. Read your policy

    Look at the deductible, the water damage wording and any seepage exclusion.

  5. Talk to your agent

    Ask about the effect on your premium and how reports versus paid claims are treated.

  6. Decide

    File if the numbers and risk justify it, or pay yourself and keep the records.

Small leaks that are rarely small

  • A slow leak under a slab that wets concrete and flooring.
  • An AC drain overflow that soaks attic insulation.
  • A water heater drip that has run for weeks.
  • A kitchen leak behind cabinets.

These tend to look minor on the surface, and many also fall under gradual damage exclusions. Document, dry and ask before assuming either way.

A worked example, in plain terms

Imagine a washing machine hose splits and wets a laundry closet and a hallway. The cleanup and repair scope comes to a figure that is only a little above your deductible. Filing may pay a small amount while adding a claim to your record.

On the other hand, imagine the same hose leak ran for a day and wet the subfloor and two rooms of carpet. The scope is several times your deductible. The balance tips toward filing.

The example is simple on purpose. Real decisions turn on your policy wording, your insurer's practices and your plans, such as selling the home or switching carriers. A quick call to your agent, framed as a question about how a hypothetical claim would be treated, is a good first step.

Document either way

Even if you do not file, keep photos, readings, invoices and notes. If the damage later proves larger or you decide to sell, a record shows what happened and what was done to fix it. Buyers and inspectors respond well to a clear repair file.

Seminole and Pinellas notes

Florida insurance has been tight in recent years, and many homeowners in Pinellas County have high deductibles, roof-age rules and named-storm percentages. A water claim can interact with those, so a short call to your agent is time well spent. Condo owners near Indian Rocks Beach or in Saint Petersburg should also ask what the association master policy covers before filing.
