How storms get water into a Seminole house

Storm water rarely arrives through one clean opening. Shingles lift in strong winds, flashing around vents fails, window seals leak under wind-driven rain, and garage doors let in surface runoff. The Atlantic hurricane season runs from June 1 to November 30, and Pinellas County is exposed to both Gulf and Bay conditions, so the rainy months produce the heaviest events.

Water that enters through a roof soaks insulation and ceilings. Water that enters at ground level travels along the slab. Each pattern needs a different drying plan.

Emergency steps after storm water gets in

  1. Make sure the area is safe. Stay clear of downed lines, wet ceilings and standing water around outlets.
  2. If a roof is open, call a roofer for a temporary tarp. We dry the interior, and a roofer closes the opening.
  3. Move furniture and valuables to dry areas and lift small items off wet floors.
  4. Photograph all damage before cleanup, including the roof if you can see it safely from the ground.
  5. Call (727) 626-0985 and describe where the water entered.

The line is answered 24/7. After a big storm many households call at once, so the dispatcher will tell you honestly how the schedule looks.

Rain, surge and the water category

Rain through a roof or window often starts as Category 1 clean water, though it passes through attic insulation and debris and can become Category 2. Surface runoff and storm surge carry soil and contaminants and are treated as Category 3. Which one applies decides whether carpet, pad and drywall can be dried or must be removed.

What the cleanup includes

  1. Extract and assess

    Standing water is removed and moisture is mapped, including inside walls and ceilings.

  2. Remove wet materials

    Insulation, ceiling tiles and drywall that stay saturated come out.

  3. Dry

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until readings reach dry targets.

  4. Coordinate repairs

    Drywall, flooring and paint are repaired, and the roof or window repair is coordinated with the right trade.

Preparing a Seminole home before storm season

  • Inspect roof flashing, vent boots and sealants each spring.
  • Clear gutters and downspouts so water leaves the foundation.
  • Know the location of the water main shutoff.
  • Keep photos of your home and valuables for any future claim.
  • Check your homeowners policy for hurricane deductibles and flood coverage.

Pinellas County publishes evacuation zones, and local officials give storm guidance. Check them before June.

Cost factors and insurance

Price follows the area wetted, the water category, the amount of material removed and the drying days. Florida homeowners policies often carry a separate percentage deductible for named storms, and flood damage is usually excluded unless a flood policy exists. Read the wording carefully and ask your insurer. Our post on steps of a water damage claim explains documentation.

The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard places rain through a roof opening in Category 1 or 2 depending on contact with insulation and debris, and treats surge as Category 3.

Safety steps after a storm

  • Stay away from downed power lines and report them to the utility.
  • Do not use a generator inside a house or garage.
  • Do not walk through floodwater, which can hide debris and live current.
  • Watch for sagging ceilings and loose roofing.
  • Wait for local officials to say it is safe to return after evacuation.
