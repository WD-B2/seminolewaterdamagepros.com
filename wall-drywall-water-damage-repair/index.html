What water does to drywall

Drywall is gypsum between paper layers. When it gets wet, the paper absorbs water, the gypsum softens, paint bubbles and the board loses strength. Water often wicks upward from the floor, so a wall can be wet a foot or more above the visible line. Behind the board, insulation and studs can stay wet long after the surface dries.

Clean water that is caught fast may be dried in place. Board that stays wet for a couple of days, or that touched dirty water, is usually cut out.

How to tell how far the damage goes

  • Soft or crumbly board when pressed with a finger.
  • Bubbling or peeling paint and cracking joints.
  • Stains with a brown tide line, which can mean repeated wetting.
  • Baseboards that swell, pull away or smell sour.
  • A musty smell near the wall base even when it looks dry.

A moisture meter reads through the surface and shows where the wet material ends. We use it and a thermal camera before we cut anything.

The restoration process

  1. Find the source

    Water must be stopped by a plumber, roofer or HVAC technician before drywall work begins.

  2. Remove wet board

    Board is cut out above the wet line, and wet insulation is removed. Cuts are straight so they are easy to patch.

  3. Dry the cavity

    Air movers and dehumidifiers run until studs, plates and remaining board reach dry targets.

  4. Rebuild

    New drywall is hung, taped, textured and primed, and painted to match the room.

Drywall in Seminole homes

Many Seminole homes use drywall on frame walls over slab floors, and some older ones use block walls with interior furring. In humid weather drywall absorbs moisture from the air as well, so a leak pushes the board past its limit faster. Bathrooms, laundry closets and kitchen exterior walls are where we see this most often. Baseboards, especially when caulked and painted tight to the floor, trap water behind them.

The EPA says mold can start on wet drywall within 24 to 48 hours. If staining or odor is already present, our mold remediation page describes the process, and our guide on how to clean mold off walls covers small surface cases.

Cost factors and insurance

Cost depends on how much board and insulation is removed, how many days of drying follow and how much taping, texture and paint the repair requires. Texture matching is a skill, and larger repairs often require repainting a full wall.

Insurers often cover sudden events and exclude gradual seepage, so read your policy and ask your insurer. Our post on how much water damage restoration costs explains the broader price drivers.
