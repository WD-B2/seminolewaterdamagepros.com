Water damage restoration in the Oakhurst subdivisions

Oakhurst is not one tract. It is a group of neighboring subdivisions in south Seminole along Oakhurst Road, including Oakhurst Shores, Oakhurst Groves, Oakhurst Terrace and Oakhurst Highlands. Public listing sources put most of the homes between 1952 and the late 1970s. The area spans ZIP codes 33772 and 33776.

Crews head out from the Park Boulevard North office, and the dispatch line is staffed 24/7. Same-day response is available when a crew is free. Call (727) 626-0985 and say where the water started and which rooms are wet. If you can reach the main valve safely, shut it off.

What 1950s to 1970s block homes mean for a water loss

Local sources describe Oakhurst Groves as mostly concrete block homes built from 1952 to 1960. Block walls do not swell the way wood framing can, but everything attached to them does. Baseboards, drywall, insulation, cabinet bases and carpet pad absorb water and hold it.

Most of these houses sit on a concrete slab. Water that reaches a slab floor spreads sideways under carpet and pad before anyone sees a stain, so the wet area is often larger than it looks. Moisture readings along walls and under flooring show how far it went, and that is what decides how much equipment the job needs.

Homes of this age often still have older water heaters in the garage, air handlers in a closet or attic and supply lines that were patched over the decades. Each of those can leak, and each ties to a service page.

Oakhurst Shores and the southern edge

One local guide counts roughly 250 single-family homes in Oakhurst Shores, in the southern part of Seminole. Homes closer to the bay side of the city can see tidal and storm-driven water that inland streets do not. Heavy rain also pools in low driveways and garages on any block when drains cannot keep up.

Storms, flooding and contaminated water

Hurricane season runs from June to November, and Pinellas County publishes evacuation zones that are worth knowing before a storm warning. Water that comes in from outside, or backs up from a drain, is usually treated as Category 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard. Porous materials such as carpet and pad normally come out in that case. See our flood damage and sewage backup pages.

After the water is out

Wet drywall and pad left damp in Florida humidity can support mold growth within a couple of days, which is the 24 to 48 hour window the EPA describes. Dehumidifiers and air movers run until readings reach dry standards, and the readings are logged. Our structural drying page explains the process, and the mold remediation page covers what happens if growth has already started.

Take photos of the damage and the source before anything is moved. Whether a loss is covered depends on your policy and the cause, so read it or ask your insurer. Our guides on claim steps and on whether homeowners insurance covers water damage explain the usual questions.
