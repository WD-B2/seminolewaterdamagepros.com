How a waterfront setting changes the work

Homes beside tidal canals deal with water from two directions. Rain and plumbing leaks come from above and inside, while high tides and storm surge can push water up from the canal and the street. Water from outside is treated as Category 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard because it carries soil, bacteria and debris.

That classification matters for cleanup. Carpet, pad, insulation and low drywall are normally removed rather than dried, and the remaining structure is cleaned and disinfected before drying starts. Our flood damage page walks through the sequence.

Salt air also wears on fittings. Hose bibs, water heater connections, air conditioner components and exposed copper corrode faster near salt water, so slow leaks are common in homes of this age. A drip behind a wall can run for weeks before a stain shows.

Flood zones and insurance

We have not verified neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status here. Zones change between lots, so check your own parcel in the FEMA Flood Map Service Center or the county flood zone lookup before assuming anything.

Waterfront owners should read their policy closely. Flood is usually excluded from a standard homeowners policy and covered by a separate flood policy, and many Florida policies carry a separate deductible for named storms. Ask your insurer which cause of loss applies before you assume anything. Our guide to homeowners insurance and water damage covers the basics.

Boats, docks and pool equipment

Many waterfront lots have docks, boat lifts and pool equipment. A failed pool line or a dock water hookup can put clean water against a foundation or slab, and the drying plan changes when the source is outside the house. Tell the dispatcher what is on the lot so the crew brings the right pumps.

After a storm

Before the first drops arrive, move valuables off the lowest floor and photograph each room. After the water recedes, avoid walking through standing canal water, and keep power off in flooded rooms until a qualified electrician says it is safe. Wet porous materials left in warm humid air can support mold growth within a couple of days, so speed matters. See our mold remediation page if growth has already started.
