Saint Petersburg is the largest city in Pinellas County. It sits on a peninsula between Tampa Bay and the Gulf, and our dispatch line covers it 24/7. Housing that shapes the work

St. Petersburg has some of the oldest housing in the county. Many neighborhoods, including Old Northeast, Historic Kenwood, Crescent Lake and Euclid-St. Paul, have 1920s and 1930s bungalows with raised pier-and-beam floors, plaster walls and original galvanized or clay plumbing.

Raised floors mean crawl space water and wet joists, and plaster walls dry differently from drywall. Mid-century ranch homes on slabs fill other parts of the city, and condo towers sit downtown and along the waterfront.

That variety means the right plan depends on the house. A raised bungalow calls for the approach on our crawl space page, a slab home may need slab leak attention and a condo needs coordination with the association.

Flooding and storms

Parts of St. Petersburg sit very low, and tidal flooding, storm surge and heavy rain overwhelm drainage in neighborhoods near the bay and Boca Ciega Bay. The 2024 hurricane season brought surge and rain to many streets, and the city and Pinellas County publish evacuation zones and recovery guidance. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your own address and ask your insurer what is covered.

Flood and sewage-contaminated water are Category 3 under the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard, which means porous materials usually come out. See our flood damage and sewage backup pages.

Cost factors and insurance

Cost depends on the water category, floor area, building type and drying days. Older homes can add complexity, such as lead paint or asbestos-era materials that require testing before demolition, which is why a pre-work assessment matters.

Insurance usually responds to sudden events, and often excludes flood and gradual seepage. Read your policy and ask your insurer. Our guide on how much water damage restoration costs covers the general price drivers.

Mold is a common follow-up in older humid houses, and our mold remediation page explains the process. For barrier island properties, see Indian Rocks Beach.

Older buildings and what we check before work

Many Saint Petersburg homes and commercial buildings date from before the late 1970s. Older paint, flooring adhesives, plaster and ductwork can contain materials that require testing before they are cut or demolished. When a wet wall or floor is that old, the plan includes a check on whether testing should come first, and the owner or property manager decides how to proceed.

Taking that step first protects the occupants and the crew.

Older plumbing is the other recurring factor. Galvanized supply lines corrode from the inside and leak at joints, and cast-iron drains crack and seep. Replacing a failed section does not always address the rest of the run, so we encourage owners to ask a plumber to look at the age of the whole system after a loss.

Safety steps and what to do while you wait

  • Shut off the water main if the source is a pipe, and keep out of flood water.
  • Leave the building if water is near the electrical panel or you smell gas.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on wet floors.
  • Photograph damage and keep damaged items for the adjuster unless they are unsafe.
